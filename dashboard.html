Today's Digest

**NBA dominates the day's biggest headlines, led by a flurry of Cavaliers moves.** Cleveland locked up James Harden on a three-year, $97 million extension and acquired forward Peyton Watson in a five-team deal involving the Nuggets and Clippers, with Watson signing a four-year, $88 million deal upon arrival. The Nuggets, who sent Watson to Cleveland, continue to draw questions about Nikola Jokić's future — a Greek club owner confirmed making a "very serious" offer for the star and pledged to pursue him again in free agency. Elsewhere, Russell Westbrook officially retired after 18 NBA seasons, and the Lakers completed a record $12.5 billion sale to Bob Iger and Josh Kushner, with the Buss family's internal dispute over the transaction still unresolved. **In MLB,** Jacob deGrom reached 2,000 career strikeouts Thursday, becoming the second-fastest pitcher to hit the milestone. The Mariners called up top pitching prospect Kade Anderson for Saturday's start against the Cubs, and the Dodgers placed closer Edwin Díaz on the injured list with an 11.85 ERA this season. **In the NHL,** the league confirmed the Bruins and Oilers will each play two regular-season games in Germany during the 2027-28 season — Bruins in Munich, Oilers in Cologne. Columbus goalie Elvis Merzlikins is out indefinitely following shoulder surgery. **NFL preseason Week 2** is underway, with the Cowboys and Saints each fined $500,000 following a series of fights at joint practice, and Sam LaPorta's Lions status for Week 1 remains uncertain after a hip injury.
